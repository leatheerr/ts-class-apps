Real-Life Conversation
You've just met a potential new client outside. Greet them, share what you do, and hand over your card.
Neighbor: Hi there! I don't think we've met.
You: Hi, I'm Julian. Nice to meet you.
Neighbor: Nice to meet you too. What do you do?
You: I work in construction. I'm a contractor.
Neighbor: Oh, interesting! Do you have a card?
You: Yes, here's my business card.
Neighbor: Great, thanks. I might need some work done soon.
You: Sounds good. Take care!

Homework

2. Practice the 4 commands with a family member: have them say point / turn on / write / thumbs up in random order — you do the action. 5 rounds.
3. Read once (don't memorize): I am · you are · he/she is — "I am Julian," "You are a client," "He is a contractor too."

Your Work Week: Describe Your Schedule Without Saying "I Am Work"
By the end of this class, you can describe your weekly routine in full sentences, catch yourself before "I am" slips in front of a verb, and confirm your availability with a client the natural way.

Vocab Race · Days of the Week
Say the days of the week out loud, fast, start to finish: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday.
Now do it again — coach says a day, you say the next one, no thinking time.
Meet Fix
Answer fast, no thinking time
"I am work Monday to Saturday."
Cue if you freeze: drop the "am" — say the whole sentence, not just the fix.
"I work Monday to Saturday."
Same fossil as "I free" → "I am free" last class — different sentence, same fix.
CORE · Round 1 — Build It
A property manager wants to book you for a job and asks about your availability. Describe your full week — workdays, day off, typical shift.
Use full Present Simple sentences. No "I am" + verb.
CORE · Round 2 — Pressure Round
Same scene — but the property manager interrupts: "Wait, so are you free Saturday or not?" Answer directly, then keep describing your schedule.
Try to land one of last class's phrases too: "Sounds good," "No problem," or "Take care."
Phrase Bank · 1 of 3
A client suggests a day and time that actually suits you. How do you confirm it?
That works for me.
Phrase Bank · 2 of 3
Someone asks for a specific day, but you need a moment before committing. What do you say?
Let me check my schedule.
Phrase Bank · 3 of 3
You need to check something before confirming a date. What do you tell the client?
I'll let you know.
Pronunciation · 3rd-Person -s · Hear It
Which one has the extra "-s" sound at the end? That's the he/she form.
Pronunciation · Connected Speech · Hear It
Both are correct — but which one sounds more natural in fast, everyday speech?
Real-Life Conversation
A client wants to book you this week and asks about your schedule.
Client: Hi Julian, are you free to take a look at my kitchen this week?
You: Let me check my schedule. I work Monday to Saturday, so weekdays are tight.
Client: What about your day off?
You: Sunday's my day off — but I could make an exception.
Client: How about Saturday morning?
You: That works for me. I start early, around 8.
Client: Perfect, I'll confirm the address.
You: Sounds good. Take care!
Comprehension Check · 1 of 2
What's Julian's usual day off?
Comprehension Check · 2 of 2
Why does Julian say weekdays are "tight"?
Homework
1. Record yourself (voice memo) describing your real weekly schedule out loud, in full sentences — no "I am" + verb.
2. Practice the 3 new phrases with a family member: they name a day/time, you respond with "That works for me," "Let me check my schedule," or "I'll let you know."
3. Notice log: catch one moment this week where "I am + verb" would slip out — text Juan the sentence you actually used instead.
