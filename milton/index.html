The one idea
Read these three out loud first. Then listen.
Same letter a. Three different sounds.

Vowels are the heart of the word — and they are sounds, not letters. The letters only tell you how it's written. They don't tell you how it's said.
The trap · spelling lies
Both are written with aw. They are not the same sound.

The symbol aw means the sound in now. The spelling aw in saw is something else entirely. Trust the symbol, never the spelling.
Anchor run · five sounds, one breath
BEAT · BIT · BAIT · BET · BAT
Run it three times. Feel your jaw drop one step at a time.
Your sound #1 · hear it first
Play it. Which one did you hear?
Your sound #1 · the mouth
Mirror out. Watch yourself say each one.
walk — jaw drops, tongue back and low, lips barely rounded. Your tongue tip does nothing.

work — the tongue curls back or bunches up in the middle. Here the R is the vowel — it isn't a separate sound added on.

Two ways to make that R. Both are correct — use the one your mouth prefers.
Your sound #1 · say it ten times
"I walk to work."
Both sounds, back to back, in one short sentence. Ten times.
Your sound #2 · hear it first
Play it. Which one did you hear?
Your sound #2 · the mouth
lock — mouth open wide, tongue low and back. Like the doctor's "aaah".

look — lips somewhat rounded, tongue high and back but relaxed, not tense.

lack — lips wide and pulled sideways, tongue low and pushed forward.

The tell: lock and lack are both low — one is back, one is front. look is the odd one: high and rounded.
Your sound #2 · say it ten times
"Look — I lock the box."
Ten times. Then swap in "lack" and hear how different it is.
