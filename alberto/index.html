Class 25
Giving advice: use should, have to and had better to tell someone what to do
By the end of this class, you can give someone real advice about their life — and defend it when they push back.
Ear Sprint · 1 of 3
Listen once. Write what you hear, then say it back with the same rhythm.
"You shoulda told me sooner."
shoulda = should have
Ear Sprint · 2 of 3
Listen once. Write what you hear, then say it back.
"You hafta try that new place downtown."
hafta = have to — here it's a strong recommendation, not an order
Ear Sprint · 3 of 3
Listen once. Write what you hear, then say it back.
"We'd better leave now or we'll miss it."
we'd better = a warning, not just advice
Warm-Up · Bad Advice Only · 1 of 3
Your friend can't sleep — and drinks coffee at 9pm.
First give the WORST possible advice out loud. Then give the good version — with "should" or "have to."
Warm-Up · Bad Advice Only · 2 of 3
Your cousin wants to get in shape but quits the gym every February.
Worst advice first. Then the real one.
Warm-Up · Bad Advice Only · 3 of 3
A friend keeps arriving at the airport 20 minutes before the flight.
Worst advice first. Then the real one.
Meet Fix
Fix it fast, out loud
"You should to sleep more."
"You should sleep more."
should + verb — no "to".
But: have TO sleep — that one keeps it. And for he/she it's has to.
Work Out The Rule
"You should take a day off."
normal advice
"You have to call her back."
stronger — a real necessity, or an enthusiastic recommendation
"We'd better leave now, or we'll miss it."
a warning — there's a consequence

CORE · The Advice Duel
Now your friend fights back: "That's too expensive." · "I don't have time." · "I've always done it this way." · "Yeah, but I like it."
Hold your ground. Use a second modal, then offer an alternative.
"I get it, but you really have to try it for one week. If that's too expensive, you should at least start with the free version — that would mitigate most of the problem. Honestly, you'd better do something, or you'll feel exactly the same next month."
Pronunciation · The Silent L
shou(l)d · wou(l)d · cou(l)d
The L is written, not spoken. Say all three, then use one in a full sentence.

Class 26
Explaining strict rules to a new hire: internal vs. external obligation — and why the rule exists
By the end of this class, you can tell someone new what they must do, what they're not allowed to do — and explain why the rule exists, without translating in your head, and without adding "to" after a modal.
Opening Drill · The Rule
After should, must, can, could, would, will — the next verb has NO "to".
Only one modal keeps "to": ought to. Say the rule out loud once, then start the drill.
Modal Drill · 1 of 5
Say it fast, no thinking time
deberías ir
You should go.
Not "should to go".
Modal Drill · 2 of 5
Say it fast, no thinking time
debemos arreglar esto
We must fix this.
Not "must to fix".
Modal Drill · 3 of 5
Say it fast, no thinking time
tienes que llamar
You have to call.
Not "have to to call".
Modal Drill · 4 of 5
Say it fast, no thinking time
deberías haberlo dicho
You should have said it.
Same family — should have + the done thing.
Modal Drill · 5 of 5 — the exception
This ONE keeps "to"
deberíamos notificarlo
We ought to notify it.
"Ought to" is the only modal that keeps "to". Every other one you just used — should, must, have to — does NOT.
Ear Sprint · 1 of 3
Listen once. Write what you hear, then say it back.
"You hafta log in before you start."
have to → "hafta"
Ear Sprint · 2 of 3
Listen once. Catch the small word in the middle.
"We're not allowed to open that."
"to" almost disappears — it sounds like "tə"
Ear Sprint · 3 of 3
Listen once. How many words do you hear after "I"?
"I called to ask about the certificate."
called_tə_ask — three words, one sound block

Meet Fix
Fix it fast, out loud
"We must to fix this."
"We must fix this." / "We have to fix this."
must + verb — no "to".
Meet Fix
Fix it fast, out loud
"I need some advices."
"I need some advice." / "I need a piece of advice."
advice has no plural — never "advices".
Meet Fix
Fix it fast, out loud
"We arrived it late."
"We arrived at the site late."
arrive AT a place — never "arrive it".
Work Out The Rule
"We must double-check the label."
we decide it — internal
"We have to submit within 30 days."
the law decides it — external
"You must submit it in order to comply."
rule + WHY = authority
CORE · The New Hire · Level 1
"Do I actually have to fill in all these forms? Can't I just do it later?"
Answer starting from a chunk — and add the WHY.
CORE · The New Hire · Level 2
"My last company didn't do it this way. That seems like a waste of time, honestly."
Hold the rule. Chunk first, then the reason.
CORE · The New Hire · Level 3
He signs a document he never verified: "Relax, nobody checks."
Confront him. Name it, and say what happens next.
It's mandatory to verify before you sign. We're not allowed to sign anything we didn't check — the rule states that every entry needs a verified source, so that the batch can be traced. If an auditor finds it, the company has to report it.
The WHY Chain
"You're required to log in."
— Why? … Why does that matter? … Why?
Five levels deep. Every answer needs "to", "in order to", or "so that". Don't repeat a reason.
Phrase Bank · 1 of 3
You need to say something isn't optional — without sounding harsh.

Phrase Bank · 2 of 3
You want to make clear the rule isn't yours — it's the authority's.
That's not our rule — it's the regulation.
Phrase Bank · 3 of 3
You're about to explain the reason behind a rule.
The reason for that is…
Final Challenge · 60-Second Induction
"Welcome to the department. Tell me the 5 rules I cannot break — and why each one exists."
5 rules from the chunks · 5 reasons · 1 prohibition · no sentence built in Spanish first.

Class 27 · CHECK
Brief a new hire on a real compliance problem — advise, require, and explain why, unscripted
By the end of this class, you can brief someone on a real compliance problem — tell them what they should do, what they have to do, and why — without notes and without being asked.
Warm-Up · 1 of 3
When you tell someone a rule, is it better to explain why or just state it? What changes?
Warm-Up · 2 of 3
This week your company had a shipment held at the border because a registration wasn't renewed. If a new hire asked you "whose fault was that?" — how would you answer, in one sentence?
Warm-Up · 3 of 3
What's one rule at your job that you think is unnecessary — and how would you argue for changing it?
🔎 Today Is A Check
This class measures — it doesn't teach.
No corrections until the end. Just do your best, the way you'd really do it at work.

Part 3 of 3 · The Real Induction
This week: a product's registration wasn't renewed because the platform failed to update — a trailer full of product sat held at the border.
You're briefing a new hire on your team about what happens when this kind of problem comes up. Juan plays the new hire. Go — no notes, no restarts.
Moment 1 of 3 · Legal vs. Internal
Explain at least one thing you have to do (the agency's rule) and one thing your team must do (your own call). The new hire will ask "says who?"
Attribute each one correctly, out loud.
Moment 2 of 3 · The Why
Every rule gets a purpose: to / in order to / so that. A rule with no reason gets pushed back on — and Juan will push back.
Moment 3 of 3 · The Advice Turn
The new hire describes a grey-area situation: "the supplier sent the data late — should I just submit without it?"
Advise using should / ought to, then state the obligation.
