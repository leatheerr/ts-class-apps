🔥 Parte 1 de 6
Intro Under Fire
Your boss meets you for the first time and fires off three fast questions.

"I'm Diego, I'm 26, I have a lot of tools."
Not "I have 26 years" — to be for age, to have for what you own.
🔥 Parte 2 de 6
The Daily Report
He wants to know what you do here, every day.

"Every day I check the blueprints, I install the units, and I clean up the site."
🔥 Parte 3 de 6
Yesterday's Work
Now he wants to know what happened yesterday — and what broke.

"Yesterday I installed two units. One unit broke because the pressure was too high."
Say the -ed out loud — don't drop it.
🔥 Parte 4 de 6
Defend A Mistake
Something didn't get finished. He wants an explanation — calm, not panicked.

"I didn't finish because the foreman didn't bring the parts on time."
🔥 Parte 5 de 6
Ask The Right Questions
Now it's your turn — you need information back from him. Ask about tomorrow's start time and where to get the parts.

"What time do we start tomorrow? Where do I pick up the parts?"
Not "What time we start?" — the question word needs do.
🔥 Parte 6 de 6
Relay A Message
He gives you a message for the crew. You have to pass it on to someone else.

"He told me we're starting at six and the inspector's coming Thursday."
Not "he tell me" or "he said me" — it's told.
Verdict
✓ Phase 1 Passed — On to Phase 2
Three months ago, a fast question from a boss made you freeze and reach for Spanish. Today you held a whole unscripted conversation, start to finish, in English.
Your next target: making it just as accurate as it is fast. Keep an ear on:
① The -ed ending on past verbs — installed, finished. Say it, don't drop it.
② "I was eating" — never "I was eat."
③ "He told me…" — never "he tell me."
Nice work, Diego.
See you in Phase 2.

Phrase Bank · 1 of 3

Pronunciation · was/were, weak forms
we were working
"were" is swallowed — /wər/, not stressed. Say it out loud.
Hear It · was/were vs simple past
"we were working"
"we worked"
Which one means the action was still going on when something else happened?
Real-Life Conversation
Your girlfriend asks why you got home at nine. Set the scene — don't just say "work was bad."
Her: Why so late?
You: It was coming down hard all afternoon, so we were working slow. Two guys called out, so we were short-handed. We were racing the clock to get the unit sealed before dark.
Comprehension Check
